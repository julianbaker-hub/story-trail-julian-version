# Layered place storytelling

A good stop is neither a plaque read aloud nor a fact detached from the street. It helps the walker connect three
layers of the same place.

## The three layers

- **What is here now:** a façade, shoreline, tree, sound, view, or movement the walker can notice.
- **How it came to be:** the decisions, labour, ecology, power, and chance that produced the present scene.
- **Why it matters now:** a tension, continuity, or question the walker can carry to the next stop.

The visible detail is the entry point, not proof that every important history is visible. Colonial naming, displaced
communities, buried waterways, and erased labour may require the story to name an absence directly.

## Editorial guardrails

- Prefer specific people, actions, and dates over a voice-of-authority summary.
- Distinguish documented fact, oral history, interpretation, and open question.
- Credit communities and sources in the editorial record.
- Avoid turning living Indigenous cultures into a historical preface to the settler city.
- End with an invitation to notice, not a quiz with one correct reaction.
