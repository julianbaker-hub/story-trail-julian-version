# Pace the walk, not just the audio

A tour is an alternating rhythm of attention. If every stop asks for a long listen, the place becomes a backdrop. If
every segment is movement, the experience loses narrative depth.

## A useful rhythm

Think about each stop as a blend of four modes:

- **Listen:** the main narrative, ideally focused on one memorable idea.
- **Look:** a specific detail in the environment that rewards lifting your eyes.
- **Move:** enough distance to reset attention and reveal the next place.
- **Breathe:** intentional quiet, especially after a dense or emotionally weighty story.

There is no universal ratio. A compact civic route can sustain denser listening than a waterfront walk. The useful test
is whether two adjacent stops compete for the same kind of attention.

## Review questions

- Does every stop earn its place with a distinct idea?
- Is there a visible detail that anchors the story in the present location?
- Do long stories have recovery time before the next arrival?
- Can a walker pause or skip without losing route context?
